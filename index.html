Beginner Forex Lessons

Learn at your own pace. Educational content only; forex trading involves risk and losses are possible.

Lesson 1: Introduction to Forex

Forex (foreign exchange) is where one currency is exchanged for another. EUR/USD compares the euro with the US dollar. EUR is the base currency and USD is the quote currency. A price of 1.0800 means one euro is priced at 1.08 US dollars. Buying a pair generally benefits if its price rises; selling generally benefits if it falls. Prices can move against you.

Quick check: In GBP/USD, which is the base currency?

Lesson 2: Reading Charts

Candlesticks show a period's opening, closing, highest and lowest prices. The body represents the open-to-close range; the wicks show the high and low. Colors vary by platform, so check its legend. A single candle cannot reliably predict the next price move. Practise reading historical charts without risking money.

Quick check: What do candle wicks show?

Lesson 3: Pips, Spreads and Lots

A pip measures price movement. For many non-JPY pairs it is the fourth decimal place; for many JPY pairs it is the second. The spread is the difference between the bid (sell) and ask (buy) prices. A lot describes trade size; larger positions generally mean price changes have a larger money impact. Costs and contract sizes vary by instrument and broker.

Quick check: What is the spread?

Lesson 4: Risk Management

Forex can cause rapid losses, especially when leverage is used. Never trade money needed for essentials or emergencies. A stop-loss is intended to close a position at a chosen level, but execution can differ during fast markets or price gaps. It does not guarantee a maximum loss. Estimate possible losses and costs before a practice trade, keep position sizes small, and beware of guaranteed-profit claims.

Quick check: Does a stop-loss guarantee the exact exit price?

Lesson 5: Demo Trading Practice

A demo account uses virtual funds to practise platform features and orders. It may not fully reproduce live-market emotions, execution, spreads or slippage. Practice plan: choose a pair, write down your reason for expecting a move, record a hypothetical entry and exit, note the result, and review what you learned. Keep a journal; you do not need to deposit money to learn basics.
